# Changelog

All notable changes to Kiosk Satellite Ground Control are documented here.

## Unreleased

### Changed
- **Learn from commands is removed.** Ground Control no longer adds commands to a person's voice on its own. A command carries whatever plays in the room under it. A voiceprint made from commands heard next to a video took the video's voice for the user's. A voice is now only made from audio you chose to add: an enrollment or a session's speech added with **Use this voice**. The **Learn from commands** switch under **Settings > Voice Match**, the `LEARN` option and the app's `learn` option are gone. If a kiosk in a new room doesn't recognize someone well, enroll once more on that kiosk.
- **Commands learned so far are deleted on the first start.** The commands that earlier versions added to voices are removed and the voiceprints of the people they were added to are built again from their enrollments alone. Enrollments and speech added with **Use this voice** are kept. The log says how many voices this changed.

### Fixed
- **A video's speech answered as a follow-up.** In a conversation next to a TV or video with a voice a little like the user's, a stretch of its speech could pass a voice check after the first command and reach the assistant as a turn of its own. With Isolation on, every turn after the first is now held until it ends, cleaned by Isolation and scored on what comes back. The user's turn goes to the assistant cleaned. Another voice's turn is left out and the kiosk is never told about it, so the assistant is not interrupted. Such a session shows **Another voice left out** under **Steps**. In 42 recorded sessions next to such a video, 29 of 31 follow-ups reached the assistant and none of the video's speech did. Before, 26 follow-ups and 7 stretches of the video did. A follow-up reaches the assistant about a second later than before. See [Another Voice After the First Command](docs/voice-match.md#another-voice-after-the-first-command).
- **A command turned away before it was said.** A kiosk that answers chosen people turned a voice away once 2 seconds of speech after the wake word had named nobody. With a TV playing, those 2 seconds could be the TV's, heard while the user waited for a realtime assistant's ready chime. When the wake word came close to one of those people, the voice is now only turned away at the end of the command or after 6 seconds of speech. With Isolation on, a command that still names nobody is run through Isolation with that person's voiceprint and scored on what comes back. Five recorded commands that were turned away next to a video score 0.54 to 0.62 this way and 0.31 to 0.50 as the kiosk heard them. See [Recognition](docs/voice-match.md#recognition).

## v2026.11.17 - 2026-11-26

### Added
- **Vulkan image for AMD GPUs (#27).** The new `vulkan` image runs the models on a GPU through Vulkan, so an AMD GPU can do what the CUDA image does on an NVIDIA one. The speaker models, Isolation, Face Detection, Hand Gestures and Face Recognition can run on the GPU. The Wake Word Aligner runs on the CPU, as in every image. Pass the GPU with `--device /dev/dri`, as in [compose.vulkan.yaml](compose.vulkan.yaml). See [AMD GPU](docs/installation.md#amd-gpu).
- **Each model runs where it is faster.** When a model loads in the Vulkan image, Ground Control runs it on the GPU and on the CPU with the same input. If the results differ or the GPU is not faster, the model runs on the CPU. **Settings** shows where the speaker model, Isolation and Vision Match run. A small integrated GPU is slower than its CPU for every model, so everything runs on the CPU there.
- **The GPU's work in helper processes.** In the Vulkan image each model runs in a helper process and Isolation is sent to the GPU in short jobs, since Linux's AMD driver cancels a job that runs for 2 seconds. If the GPU is lost anyway, a voice check or a look that was under way is done on the CPU. A command Isolation was working on goes to the assistant as Trimming left it and its session shows **Isolation not used** under **Steps**. The helper process starts again and the model is back on the GPU a few seconds later.
- **Accurate vision models by default on a fast GPU in the Vulkan image.** At start, Ground Control times the Accurate Face Detection and Hand Gestures models on the GPU. A feature whose Accurate model takes 50 ms or less there is Accurate by default. On an RTX 4000 they take 13 and 26 ms, so both are Accurate. On a slower GPU the defaults are the CPU image's. A model chosen under **Settings > Vision Match** stays as chosen.
- **`VULKAN_DEVICE`.** In the Vulkan image, set it to part of a GPU's name to choose between several GPUs.
- **Vulkan image times and tests.** On an RTX 4000 through Vulkan, a 5 second command waits 0.12 s for Isolation, a 1 second voice check takes 7 ms and a Vision Match look with the Light models takes 27 ms where the CPU takes 62 ms. A look with the Accurate models takes 52 ms. The 2,357 frames of the kiosk test set read the same as on the CPU and 120 recorded voice sessions gave the same events. On Mesa's RADV driver with the integrated Radeon of a Ryzen 9 9950X3D, every model gives the CPU's results. The image has not been tested on a Radeon card yet.

### Fixed
- **Runs on in Kiosk Satellite with the OpenVINO and Vulkan images.** Kiosk Satellite's Ground Control page showed **CUDA** whenever the speaker model ran on a GPU and **CPU** otherwise, so the Vulkan image showed as CUDA and the OpenVINO image as CPU. Ground Control now tells each kiosk whether its models reach a GPU through CUDA, OpenVINO or Vulkan. Needs the next Kiosk Satellite release to show it.
- **Runs on under Isolation and Vision Match in Settings.** In the OpenVINO and Vulkan images the line under **Runs on** named the CUDA option. It now says where the models run in that image.

## v2026.11.16 - 2026-11-26

### Added
- **Each kiosk's own Wake Word Aligner switch.** A Kiosk Satellite that has **Enable Wake Word Aligner** sends it with each session. The Wake Word Aligner runs when it is on there and in Ground Control. A kiosk's page shows it under **Voice Match**.
- **HA_TOKEN (#28).** Set `HA_TOKEN` to a long-lived Home Assistant access token and the web UI opens without logging in with Home Assistant, so there is no login and no two-factor code each time. Anyone who can reach port 10900 can then open Ground Control, and the installation page warns about it. Kiosks still sign in with their own token. If Home Assistant refuses the token, the Sign in page says so and shows **Log in with Home Assistant** instead. Ground Control's log says at the start whether the token opens the web UI. See [Skip the Login](docs/installation.md#skip-the-login).

### Changed
- **Driver for older Intel GPUs in the OpenVINO image (#25).** The image now also has Intel's driver for the integrated GPUs of 5th to 10th generation Core processors, such as the UHD Graphics 630. Without it the image stopped with `OpenVINO found no Intel GPU` on those machines. These GPUs are not tested yet.
- **Install instructions grouped by image.** The README's Docker section and the Docker part of the installation page now start with a table of the three images. The CPU, the NVIDIA GPU and the Intel GPU each have a section with the command and what that image needs. GPU Memory sits under NVIDIA GPU.
- **README badges.** The NVIDIA badge now reads CUDA without its versions. An Intel OpenVINO badge links to the Intel GPU section of the installation page. The Docker badge is purple.

### Fixed
- **Asked to log in again when Home Assistant restarts (#28).** The web UI showed the Sign in page whenever Ground Control couldn't reach Home Assistant to check the browser's token, such as during a restart or an update. Logging in again then meant entering the two-factor code each time. The web UI now shows that Home Assistant can't be reached and tries again every 5 seconds, then opens the page once Home Assistant answers. The browser keeps its login. An error page from a proxy in front of Home Assistant no longer signs the browser out either. The Sign in page only shows when Home Assistant refuses the login.
- **Signed out in Safari after a week away.** Safari deletes what a page stores after 7 days without a visit, and the web UI kept its login there. The login is now an HttpOnly cookie that Ground Control sets, which Safari keeps, and page scripts can't read it. The cookie lasts 90 days from the last visit, as long as Home Assistant keeps an unused login. A browser that logged in before the update moves its login to the cookie the first time it opens Ground Control, without logging in again. Behind an HTTPS reverse proxy that sends `X-Forwarded-Proto`, the cookie is only sent over HTTPS.
- **Near silence sent to the assistant for some short commands.** Isolation now and then took the user for absent in a command of about a second and returned it 40 dB or more quieter, so the assistant heard nothing. A turn Isolation returns more than 20 dB quieter than it went in now goes to the assistant as Trimming left it. The session's page shows **Isolation not used** under **Steps** and the session's line in the log says so. On our own kiosks this was 6 of 28 short commands in a day.
- **Rejected after a pause with Talk right after the wake word.** With **Talk right after the wake word** on, the last sounds of the wake word were taken for the start of the command. A user who waited a second before talking then had a kiosk that answers chosen people reject the session, since that sliver of speech was too short to recognize anyone by. The end of the wake word no longer counts as speech. In our test sessions this was 14 of 27 commands on one kiosk.

## v2026.11.15 - 2026-11-25

### Added
- **Vision Match: Face Detection and Hand Gestures.** Ground Control can now process a kiosk's camera frames. It finds faces and whether they look at the kiosk, meaning the whole face is in the frame, the head is turned 30 degrees or less to either side and the face isn't seen from far below or above. It also counts the fingers held up on a raised hand. Kiosks with Vision Match on send their frames over a new socket, `/api/vision`, instead of running these models on the device. Choose Light or Accurate for each feature under **Settings > Vision Match**. Light is SCRFD 2.5G for faces and YOLOv10n trained on HaGRIDv2 gestures for hands. Accurate is SCRFD 10G and YOLOv10x. Every feature defaults to Accurate on an NVIDIA GPU and to Light on a CPU, except Face Recognition (see below). On kiosk frames at 1 to 3 m, Light got the finger count right in 91% of frames and Accurate in 96%. The models Kiosk Satellite runs on the device got 28%. Both told a head turned away from the screen apart from one looking at it in 90% of frames. On the device it was 18%. The models load when the first kiosk requests them. Ground Control measures how many looks a second it keeps up with and sends that rate to each kiosk. The CPU image and the Home Assistant app include every model. Needs the upcoming Kiosk Satellite release with Vision Match.
- **Vision sessions.** Vision Match looks are saved as sessions on the **Sessions** page alongside the voice sessions. A vision session covers one stretch of time someone is in front of a kiosk. It starts with the first face or hand and ends 10 seconds after the last one or when the kiosk's camera stops. Looks that find nothing open no session, so the list doesn't fill up with empty stretches. A vision session's page has a timeline of what the kiosk did (the screensaver dismissed or postponed, a finger gesture and the action it ran) and every look with the face, the hand and its finger count and the time it took. The page also lists the steps in order and shows the median, 95th percentile and slowest time of each model and of the round trip from the kiosk. It shows how long each gesture took to run its action and up to 8 frames with the faces and the hand drawn on them. The frames include the one each gesture acted on. **Download** saves it as a zip with its frames. The Sessions list has a **Type** filter. The Overview counts vision sessions. See [Vision Sessions](docs/sessions.md#vision-sessions).
- **Keep frames and Delete all frames.** Both are under **Settings > Vision Match > Frames**. Keep frames is on by default and keeps up to 8 camera frames for each vision session. Delete all frames deletes every kept frame at once but keeps the sessions.
- **Vision Match on a kiosk's page.** The new **Vision Match** section shows the kiosk's Vision Match features the same way **Voice Match** does: whether Face Detection and Hand Gestures run on Ground Control or on the kiosk, Dismiss on face, Postpone on face and the finger gestures set up. **What it sees** shows each look of the current vision session as it happens. It shows the face from the last face look and the hand from the last hand look. **Looks** counts the kiosk's looks, empty looks and vision sessions for each of the last 7 days. **Events** lists vision sessions starting and ending, the screensaver dismissed and each finger gesture.
- **Face Recognition.** Ground Control can now recognize who is looking at a kiosk using the faces enrolled on the **People** page. Each kiosk shows the result in its new Face sensor in Home Assistant. A face is enrolled through a kiosk's camera in a guided capture of about half a minute (look at the screen, turn a little to each side, take a step back). Start it on the kiosk or from the person's page. Only small pictures of the face are kept, so changing the vision model rebuilds the faces without anyone enrolling again. A face is named after two looks in a row above the threshold or one very close match. It is then checked again every 2 seconds. Light uses ArcFace with a MobileFaceNet backbone (17 ms a face on an Intel N100). Accurate uses ArcFace R50 (3.5 ms on an NVIDIA GPU). On kiosk frames 1 to 3 meters away in bright and dim light, the enrolled person was recognized in every frame where their face looked at the kiosk. In simulated households of six people, nobody was ever taken for someone else. Set the threshold under **Settings > Vision Match > Face Recognition**. Vision sessions show who was recognized. Needs the Kiosk Satellite release with Vision Match. See [Face](docs/people.md#face).
- **Several people in front of a kiosk.** Up to three faces looking at a kiosk are now recognized at once and each one is tracked from look to look. The person at the kiosk is the one with the highest score. It changes only after someone else scores higher two checks in a row or the person leaves, so two people side by side don't make it flip. Kiosks receive everyone recognized along with the person at the kiosk for their new **People in view** sensor. The kiosk page and vision sessions show the others in view.
- **Confirm voices with faces.** With Confirm voices with faces on in Kiosk Satellite, a voice under the threshold is accepted as the person recognized at that kiosk when their voiceprint is also the closest and the score reaches a seventh of the way from the other-voice line to the threshold. A face alone is never enough to name a voice. The session page and the log show when a voice was accepted this way. See [Confirmed by the Face](docs/voice-match.md#confirmed-by-the-face).
- **Stop a face capture from the person's page.** The capture's progress on a person's page now has a **Stop** button, which stops it on the kiosk and keeps the pictures from a finished first step.
- **Hand sensitivity.** A new setting under **Settings > Vision Match > Hand Gestures**, from 1 to 100, sets the smallest hand counted. At the default of 50 a hand has to be about 9% of the frame on its longer side. Lower it if a hand that is only a small part of the frame, such as one cut off at its edge, shows up in vision sessions. A hand under the minimum counts as no hand, so it opens no vision session and doesn't reach the kiosk's gestures. See [Hand Gestures](docs/vision-match.md#hand-gestures).
- **Kiosk PIN and Lockdown Mode in vision sessions.** A vision session now shows when a kiosk skipped its kiosk or lockdown PIN for a recognized person and when it turned Lockdown Mode off or on because of who was recognized. This needs a Kiosk Satellite version with these settings.
- **Face Recognition is not a lock.** The People docs explain that a photo of an enrolled person can be recognized as them and that the Kiosk Satellite settings that act on faces are for convenience. See [Not a Lock](docs/people.md#not-a-lock).
- **Faces and hands at the same time in a look.** In a look for both, Hand Gestures runs beside Face Detection and Face Recognition, so the look takes about as long as the slower side and not the sum of the two. On a GPU, a face model session is kept for each frame shape, up to four. Without that, a kiosk in landscape and a kiosk in portrait looking in turn took 85 ms for each face look on an RTX 4000 instead of 4 ms, because a GPU session sets up again every time its input changes shape. Each extra shape takes about 0.05 GB of GPU memory.
- **`KEEP_GPU_MEMORY` covers Vision Match.** On a GPU, each vision model hands the memory of a run back when the run is done, so Vision Match keeps about 0.25 GB instead of 0.5 GB. With `KEEP_GPU_MEMORY` on, the models keep that memory and a look with every feature takes 17 ms instead of 21 ms on an RTX 4000 and 17 ms instead of 31 ms on a GB10, where taking GPU memory again is slow.
- **Vision Match in the README and its benchmarks.** The README describes Face Detection, Hand Gestures and Face Recognition and has Vision Match benchmarks for the same machines as Voice Match's, the Raspberry Pi 4, the Intel N100, the i9-13900H, the MacBook Pro M5, the RTX 3050, the RTX 4000 SFF Ada and the GB10: the time for one look for each feature and for all of them, the looks a second each machine keeps up with and the GPU memory. With each machine's default models, a look with every feature takes 671 ms on a Pi 4, 292 ms on an N100, 64 ms on the i9 and 70 ms on the MacBook Pro and 17 to 34 ms on the GPUs with `KEEP_GPU_MEMORY` on. Vision Match's Accurate models keep about 0.25 GB of GPU memory on top of Voice Match's, or 0.5 GB with `KEEP_GPU_MEMORY`.
- **Vision Match across the docs.** Installation compares the time a Vision Match look takes on each kind of machine and says where the vision models are chosen. Kiosks, People, Sessions, Logs and Backup and Moving now cover Vision Match too: approval, Face Recognition on a kiosk's page and its events, enrolling a face when adding a person, the vision models on a vision session's page, the Vision Match log lines and the `faces/` and `faceprints/` folders in the data folder.
- **Vision Match in Getting Started.** The Getting Started guide and the README's Getting started steps now cover turning on Vision Match on the kiosks and enrolling a face. See [Getting Started](docs/getting-started.md).
- **Vision Match docs.** A new [Vision Match](docs/vision-match.md) page covers the kiosk settings, how looks are sent and paced, Face Detection, Hand Gestures, Face Recognition, the vision models with the time a look takes on each machine and every setting. The Vision Match settings moved there from the Settings page. Its links into the Kiosk Satellite docs point to Kiosk Satellite's own Vision Match page. It also covers Kiosk Satellite's Presence sensors and Finger gestures for.
- **A Vision Match model for each feature.** Face Detection, Hand Gestures and Face Recognition each have their own Light or Accurate choice under **Settings > Vision Match** in place of one choice for all three. Each shows its time on this machine once the models load. Face Recognition defaults to Accurate on x86 CPUs and Apple M-series chips, since a recognized face is checked only every 2 seconds: it takes 173 ms a face on an N100. Face Detection and Hand Gestures stay Light on a CPU and a Raspberry Pi uses Light for everything. A setting saved with the old single choice carries over to all three. A face not named yet is checked on every look for its first 2 seconds and once a second after that, so someone walking up to the kiosk is named by the first look that is good enough and a guest standing there costs much less. The **Speaker model** on the Voice Match settings page now uses the same Light and Accurate selector and says which model is the default on this machine. The Overview shows the models Face Detection, Hand Gestures and Face Recognition use.
- **OpenVINO image for Intel GPUs.** A new image, `ghcr.io/jxlarrea/kiosk-satellite-ground-control:openvino`, runs Vision Match's models on an Intel GPU with OpenVINO. The GPU can be integrated or an Arc card, from the 11th generation of Core processors or newer, which includes the Intel N100. A look with every feature takes 81 ms instead of 292 ms on an Intel N100 and 25 ms instead of 64 ms on an i9-13900H with an Iris Xe. Voice Match runs on the CPU in this image as in the CPU image, where it is as fast or faster. Start it with `--device /dev/dri` or with [compose.openvino.yaml](compose.openvino.yaml). Each model is compiled for the GPU the first time it runs and kept in the `openvino` folder of the data volume. `OPENVINO_DEVICE` picks the GPU on a machine with more than one. See [Intel GPU](docs/installation.md#intel-gpu).
- **Intel GPU benchmarks in the README.** The Vision Match benchmarks have a table for the OpenVINO image on an Intel N100 and on an Iris Xe, measured the same way as the others.

### Changed
- **Voice Isolator 1.3 takes half the time.** Isolation now takes 2.68 s instead of 5.71 s on an Intel N100, 0.57 s instead of 1.21 s on an i9-13900H, 0.37 s instead of 0.78 s on a MacBook Pro M5 and 0.06 s instead of 0.11 s on an RTX 4000 SFF Ada and a GB10. Cleaned commands sounded the same as with 1.2 in listening tests on real kiosk recordings. More of a TV is removed in recordings where the user says nothing. No one has to enroll again.
- **Less GPU memory for Isolation.** A command of 10 seconds or longer now needs up to 0.8 GB more GPU memory while it runs instead of 1.5 GB, so the CUDA image needs 2 GB of free GPU memory with Isolation instead of 2.5 GB. With `KEEP_GPU_MEMORY` on, Isolation keeps about 0.75 GB more between commands instead of 2 GB.
- **README benchmarks measured again.** Isolation's times on the Raspberry Pi 4, Intel N100, i9-13900H, MacBook Pro M5, RTX 4000 SFF Ada and GB10 and the GPU Memory tables are measured again with Voice Isolator 1.3 the same way as before. The docs, the Isolation setting and the log line for Isolation on a CPU give the new waits.
- **Plainer wording in the app and the docs.** Sentences that ended in a phrase tacked on after a comma, or that had a comma before "and" or "or", are rewritten throughout the web app, its error messages, its log lines and the docs.
- **The People page, one card per feature.** A person's page now has a **Voice Match** section and a **Vision Match** section with the same layout. Each has a title showing the person's status and a card below it. The card has an **Enroll on a kiosk** row with the kiosk and the button, what is enrolled and a list of recordings or captures that opens when needed. The long **Devices** list is now a grid of **Device models** inside the Voice Match card that shows the seconds enrolled on each. **Enroll voice** moved there from the bottom of the page. **Add from a session** is gone because **Use this voice** on a session's page does the same. The header shows whether the voice and the face are enrolled. The list of people shows both under each name. The person's page shows a face capture's progress even when it was started on the kiosk. See [People](docs/people.md).
- **Sessions in a database.** Sessions are now kept in an SQLite database called `sessions.db` in the data folder instead of one JSON file per session. The Sessions list now loads one page at a time and filters on the server, so it stays fast with thousands of sessions. On the first start, the sessions saved by earlier versions are imported and their JSON files are moved to `sessions/imported-json`. Audio and frames stay in `sessions/`. Ground Control keeps up to 2,000 voice sessions and 3,000 vision sessions, so a busy week of vision sessions never pushes voice sessions out. See [Backup and Moving](docs/backup.md).
- **Kiosk Satellite's settings paths.** The docs and the app now point to Voice Match and Vision Match under **Settings > Ground Control** in Kiosk Satellite. That is where the upcoming Kiosk Satellite release puts them.

## v2026.11.14 - 2026-11-23

### Changed
- **Limits of a short answer in the docs.** The Voice Match page now says what a short answer can't do: it is accepted without a name, a short reply from someone the kiosk doesn't answer can get through now and then, the right person's reply can still be dropped with a TV on and less than 0.2 seconds of speech is never accepted. It also suggests a reply of two seconds or more for a question where it matters who answers. See [Limits of a Short Answer](docs/voice-match.md#limits-of-a-short-answer).

### Fixed
- **Not starting on an older x86 CPU.** On a CPU without SSE4.2, or in a virtual machine with a CPU type like `kvm64` that hides it, Ground Control stopped at startup with "NumPy was built with baseline optimizations: (X86_V2) but your machine doesn't support: (X86_V2)". NumPy 2.4 and later need those instructions, so the images now come with NumPy 2.3.

## v2026.11.13 - 2026-11-23

### Added
- **Take short answers.** A new setting under **Settings > Voice Match > Recognition**, on by default. Off, a short reply to a question from Home Assistant is dropped on a kiosk that answers chosen people, as before this version.
- **How a turn started on a session's page.** With Kiosk Satellite 2026.11.31 or later, a session shows whether the turn was a reply to `ask_question` or `start_conversation`, a follow-up or started by hand.

### Fixed
- **Answers to `assist_satellite.ask_question` always dropped as an unknown voice (#20).** On a kiosk that answers chosen people, a question from Home Assistant starts a turn with no wake word. A short answer like "yes" was then dropped without its voice being checked: under a second of speech was never enough to recognize anyone. `assist_satellite.start_conversation` had the same problem. A short reply now goes through when it sounds like one of the people the kiosk answers. It is scored on 1.5 seconds centered on it, since one word on its own is too little to tell a voice by. Up to 0.75 seconds of speech needs a score just over the **Other voice threshold**, a seventh of the way to the **Threshold** (0.32 with the Accurate model's defaults), more speech needs more and from 2 seconds on the answer is recognized as before. Of 44 real short commands replayed this way in a quiet room, all 44 went through and 13 did before. A reply that reaches the **Threshold** scored this way is recognized by name: 37 of those 44 were, against 15 from their speech alone, and none of 606 replies from other voices. Under the **Threshold**, a short answer is taken without a name, so the Speaker sensor still only names someone recognized at it. Kiosk Satellite 2026.11.31 or later says how each turn started, so only a reply to something Home Assistant asked and a follow-up can be a short answer. With an older Kiosk Satellite, every turn without a wake word can be one. See [Short Answers](docs/voice-match.md#short-answers).
- **A short command from an enrolled person turned away as an unknown voice now and then.** A command under two seconds is recognized from the speech of the wake word and the command put together. That can fall short of the **Threshold** for a voice that passes it when the command is scored as it was heard: of 50 real short commands after a wake word, one scored 0.44 and was turned away on a kiosk that answers chosen people, where the same command with the room around it scores 0.54. Before a voice is left unknown or turned away, the first turn is now scored once more that way. All 50 are recognized, and none of them on a kiosk that only answers other people.
- **A session ending on a click with Answer set to Anyone.** With no wake word on a kiosk that answers anyone, a single 32 ms of speech followed by silence was taken for the user's voice. The speaker model can't take audio that short, so the session failed and the answer after it was lost. A sound that short is no longer taken for a voice.

## v2026.11.12 - 2026-11-23

### Added
- **Wake Word Aligner (#19).** With **Talk right after the wake word** on in Kiosk Satellite, the end of the wake word reached the assistant in front of the command, like the "xa" of "Alexa". Speech-to-text then heard an extra word: "So what time is it". For kiosks with Trimming on, the end of the wake word is now found and left out. A command said in one breath with the wake word gets half a second of silence in front. It works with every wake word and every wake word engine, since only the wake word's text is needed, pronounced in English and in the kiosk's language. It takes 4 ms on a fast x86 CPU and 50 ms on a Raspberry Pi 4, once per command, and doesn't delay the command. The README's performance tables have its time on every machine. When the end of the wake word isn't clear, nothing is left out. **Use Wake Word Aligner** is on by default under **Settings > Voice Match** and needs Kiosk Satellite 2026.11.30 or later. See [Wake Word Aligner](docs/voice-match.md#wake-word-aligner).
- **The wake word, its engine and the assistant on a session's page.** With Kiosk Satellite 2026.11.30 or later, a session shows the wake word and the engine that heard it, whether an Assist pipeline or a realtime assistant answered and whether **Talk right after the wake word** was on. **Steps** shows where the wake word ended when its end was left out. The session's record keeps them too, with the wake word model and the kiosk's language.

### Changed
- **espeak-ng in the images.** The Docker images and the Home Assistant app now include espeak-ng, which gives the pronunciation of a wake word. Ground Control run with Python from the source needs it installed for the Wake Word Aligner. The setting says so when it is missing.
- **Wake Word Aligner model.** A new 10 MB model, `models/wake_word_aligner.onnx`, gives the phonemes in the wake word and the start of the command. It always runs on the CPU. It has its own license, with the same terms as the Voice Isolator's. See `models/wake_word_aligner.LICENSE.md`.
- **Voice Isolator license.** The Voice Isolator models have their own license instead of CC BY-NC-ND 4.0, which allowed anyone to share them on their own. They may only be used as part of Ground Control with Kiosk Satellite and may not be redistributed apart from Ground Control, converted, modified or used to train other models. See `models/voice_isolator.LICENSE.md`. The models' own metadata names the new license too, and their weights are the same. The rest of Ground Control stays under CC BY-NC-ND 4.0.

## v2026.11.11 - 2026-11-22

### Added
- **Audio levels in a session (#17).** Under each spectrogram, a session shows how loud that audio was: its peak, the speech, the average and the noise floor in dBFS, and how far the speech stood over the noise floor in dB. A peak at 0 dBFS shows as clipped. Click any of them for all the levels and what each one means.

## v2026.11.8 - 2026-11-22

### Added
- **`KEEP_GPU_MEMORY` setting for the CUDA image.** With it on, Isolation keeps its GPU memory between commands instead of handing it back after each one, about 2 GB more. Taking that memory again for every command costs about 0.05 s on a GB10 and 0.01 s on an RTX 4000. On a GB10 the benchmark's Isolation time goes from 0.16 s to 0.11 s. It is off by default, for GPUs shared with an LLM or speech-to-text.

### Changed
- **Faster Isolation on an NVIDIA GPU.** Nearly every command has a length the GPU hasn't just seen, and for each one it set up the Voice Isolator's 130 convolutions again, which took longer than the Isolation itself. 128 of them are 1x1 convolutions, the same thing as a matrix multiplication, and the model now has them as that, with the same weights. Over 86 real commands, Isolation went from a median of 0.23 s to 0.08 s on an RTX 4000 and from 0.16 s to 0.13 s on a GB10. The output is the same model's: identical on a CPU, and on a GPU within 5 of the 65,536 steps of 16-bit audio, less than the same model already differs between a GPU and a CPU. The Voice Isolator stays at version 1.2.
- **Less CPU while someone talks.** The speaker model's worker threads kept spinning between voice checks, so a kiosk that was talking took far more CPU than its checks need. They now stop when a check ends. While a command comes in, Ground Control takes 0.24 cores instead of 2.75 on an i9-13900H with Accurate, 0.11 instead of 1.05 on an Intel N100 with Light and 0.40 instead of 0.62 on a Raspberry Pi 4 with Light. A check takes the same time as before.
- **Less GPU memory.** The CUDA image keeps about 0.7 GB on the GPU, or 0.9 GB with Isolation, and needs 1 GB of free GPU memory, or 2.5 GB with Isolation, instead of 3 GB and 4 GB. The memory it kept used to grow in use, past 2 GB with several kiosks talking and to about 6 GB while four commands ended at the same moment. Every run of a GPU model now goes through one thread, the speaker model hands its memory back after every audio length but the 1 s voice check and commands that end together are cleaned one after the other. A voice check takes the same time and other lengths about 1 ms more.
- **A faster end of the command.** When a command's first turn ended, the whole command went through the speaker model again, also when the user was already recognized and nothing used the result. That is skipped now. The command ends 0.07 s sooner on an i9-13900H, 0.4 s sooner on a Raspberry Pi 4 with Light, 0.5 s sooner on an Intel N100 with Accurate and up to 0.2 s sooner on a GPU for a command longer than 4 s.
- **README benchmarks measured again.** On the Raspberry Pi 4, Intel N100, i9-13900H, MacBook Pro M5, RTX 4000 and GB10, the same way as before. Trimming now takes 0.02 s or less everywhere but on an Intel N100 with Accurate, where it went from 0.70 s to 0.14 s. The GB10 is measured with `KEEP_GPU_MEMORY` on, where Isolation takes 0.11 s. Without it Isolation takes 0.16 s there since 2026.11.5, when it started handing its GPU memory back after each command.
- **README Downloads badge counts every image.** It showed only the Docker image's downloads and left out the Home Assistant app images for amd64 and aarch64, more than half of the total. A workflow now adds up all three every 6 hours and the badge shows the sum. The badge links to the repository's list of packages.

### Fixed
- **Short messages showing as plain text under the footer (#14).** Messages such as "Saved", "Copied" or "not enough speech in the recording" after adding a session's voice to a person appeared as small plain text at the bottom left of the page, under the footer, where they were easy to miss. A stray character in the stylesheet made browsers drop their style. They float over the bottom of the window again and go away after a few seconds.

## v2026.11.1 - 2026-11-20

The first release of Kiosk Satellite Ground Control, a companion service for Kiosk Satellite that runs the voice and vision work too heavy for a kiosk on a computer or server in your home.

### Added
- **Voice Match: Recognition, Trimming and Isolation.** Who is speaking, and only their voice in the command.
- **Recognition.** Each voice command is checked against the people enrolled, and the kiosk knows who is speaking about a second in, while the person is still talking. A kiosk can answer anyone or only the people you choose. Each kiosk gets a Speaker sensor in Home Assistant, and an LLM conversation agent can tell who is speaking from the instructions on the Settings page, so "my calendar" or "my list" means the speaker's.
- **Trimming.** The kiosk sends everything its microphone hears and gets back only the speech of the person who said the wake word. A TV or another person before, between and after their words is cut out, long pauses are shortened and the command ends once the user stops talking, whatever the TV does. A follow-up in a conversation carries on with the same person, so a one word answer like "yes" goes through. Once the user is done, a voice has to sound clearly like them to start another turn, so a TV that briefly sounds a little like them can't interrupt a realtime answer.
- **Isolation.** Other voices talking over the user's words are removed too, using the user's voiceprint, once the user stops talking. It needs the Accurate speaker model and is off until turned on under **Settings > Voice Match**, and each kiosk can turn it off in Kiosk Satellite. While a turn is held for it, a little silence keeps the audio stream open, so speech-to-text that gives up on a quiet stream, like Home Assistant Cloud's, still gets the command. A 5 s command takes about 0.1 s on an NVIDIA RTX 4000 SFF Ada and about 1.2 s on four cores of an i9-13900H. On a CPU it runs a version of the model made for the CPU, twice as fast and nearly identical.
- **Two speaker models.** Accurate (ReDimNet2 B3, multilingual) is the default on an NVIDIA GPU and on x86 CPUs with more than 4 cores. Light (ReDimNet2 B1) is the default on ARM and on smaller x86 CPUs such as the Intel N100. Changing the model rebuilds everyone's voiceprint from the kept enrollment audio in the background, so nobody records again.
- **People.** Add, rename and forget people, and give each a ring color, a to-do list, calendar, playlist and Home Assistant person, which Kiosk Satellite uses when that person says "my". The fields suggest Home Assistant's entities and Music Assistant's playlists as they are typed in. A person linked to a Home Assistant person shows that person's picture.
- **Guided enrollment on a kiosk.** Start an enrollment from the app and the person reads aloud to a kiosk of the kind they talk to. The kiosk and the app show the same text, filling as it is spoken, so the person can read from a phone while walking around the room. Speech that doesn't sound like the person, like a TV, is left out of the voiceprint. Only kiosks with Allow enrollment here on are offered.
- **Kiosks.** Kiosks on the same network find Ground Control on their own, at the machine's address on its main network, and sign in with the Home Assistant token they already have. For kiosks that can't reach that address, like kiosks on another VLAN, **mdns_address** in the app's options or `MDNS_ADDRESS` in Docker sets the address they get. A new kiosk waits for approval on the Kiosks page. If a kiosk answers chosen people Ground Control doesn't have, as after moving to another Ground Control, its page and the log say so, and a rejected command says the voice isn't one of the people that kiosk answers. Each kiosk's page shows its connection, its Kiosk Satellite version, the Voice Match features it has on, who is talking right now with each check as it happens, and its latest sessions.
- **Saved sessions.** Each session keeps its events, every score, why it ended, its audio as heard and as sent and the wake word, for 7 days by default. The session page shows the waveform colored by who was talking and every step (Isolation included), and a session's voice can be added to a person. **Download** saves a session as one zip for an issue report: its audio as heard, as sent and the wake word, its record, and Ground Control's details with the log from while it ran. Keeping the audio can be turned off.
- **The web app.** A Ground Control panel in Home Assistant's sidebar as a Home Assistant app, or its own page with **Log in with Home Assistant** as a container. Pages for Overview, People, Kiosks, Sessions, Settings and Logs. **Logs** shows what Ground Control has been doing and downloads a report for an issue, with tokens left out.
- **Home Assistant app and Docker images.** One CPU image for the app and standalone Docker (amd64 and arm64), and a CUDA 13 image for NVIDIA GPUs, which needs an NVIDIA driver for CUDA 13 (580 or newer). `compose.yaml` and `compose.cuda.yaml` run them with Docker Compose. As an app, people, voiceprints and sessions are in the app's own folder, which the Samba share and File editor apps show, and a Home Assistant backup of the app keeps them. The app's Info page shows the full Kiosk Satellite Ground Control logo, its options have names and descriptions on its Configuration tab, and Home Assistant's watchdog can restart it if it stops answering. The models are in the repository, each with its license next to it, and the service checks each one against its pinned hash when it loads.
